Refurbish only when the economics clear.

IALS does not blindly overhaul shelves of bearings. We search demand first, match exact inventory, obtain inspection/OH pricing and TAT, model BER and documentation cost, then authorize repair only when the spread and compliance path make sense.

1 · Exact identity

P/N, alternate P/N, NSN, manufacturer markings, serial/lot and physical quantity.

5 · Compliance gate

Classification, source approval, trace, end-user/end-use, destination, licensing and human release before commitment.

6 · Cash conversion

Verified demand → repair authorization → documented condition → human-approved quote → PO → collection.

2026 T56 sustainment signal

A major repair ecosystem.

The U.S. Air Force awarded a multi-award T56 Joint Follow-On Contract in July 2026 for repair and overhaul of non-commercial aircraft parts supporting T56 engines on C-130 aircraft. IALS treats that as a strong sustainment and MRO-market signal—not as proof that any particular IALS bearing is contract-eligible.

What this means commercially

  • Long-duration T56 repair/overhaul demand exists.
  • Authorized MRO and sustainment supply chains need material, repair capability and reclamation options.
  • Legacy cores can have value when exact identity, trace and acceptance requirements line up.

What it does NOT mean

  • No uploaded bearing is automatically approved for that contract.
  • No serial, cross-reference or notebook heading proves source approval or flight eligibility.
  • Critical-safety, approved-source, replacement-material and export rules must be checked per exact item.
